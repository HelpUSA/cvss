## 1. Executive Summary & Regulatory Purpose

This memorandum provides a rigorous legal and regulatory evaluation regarding the ethics oversight requirements for the HelpUS CVSS AI expert validation protocol under United States federal regulations, specifically Title 45 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Part 46 (45 CFR 46, known as the "Common Rule") and Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regulations (21 CFR Parts 50 and 56).

The fundamental determination established herein is:
1. **No Statutory Mandate for Commercial US IRB**: The research conducted by HelpUS LLC is privately funded, non-biomedical, non-clinical, and receives no direct or indirect federal funding from HHS or any of the 20 federal departments signatory to the Common Rule. Therefore, 45 CFR § 46.101 does not mandate commercial Institutional Review Board (IRB) review under US federal law.
2. **Minimal Risk & Survey Nature**: The research protocol strictly evaluates expert consensus on algorithmic cybersecurity vulnerability severity metrics (CVSS 4.0 vectors) derived from public synthetic and published benchmark records. The human expert participants evaluate technical metrics, not personal health, psychiatric, or sensitive personal attributes. Under 45 CFR § 46.104(d)(2), such technical survey research would qualify for exemption even if conducted under federal purview.
3. **Primary Coordinating Jurisdiction**: The Principal Investigator (PI, Wagner Santos) coordinates the scientific research from Brazil. Consequently, the research is submitted to primary ethics review under the Brazilian National Research Ethics System (SINEP / CEP) pursuant to Resolutions CNS 510/2016 (Humanities and Social Sciences / Professional Consultation) and CNS 466/2012.
4. **International Harmonization**: Data protection aligns concurrently with the Brazilian General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Lei 13.709/2018), US State-level Privacy Standards (e.g., California CCPA/CPRA), and the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR Art. 89) for cross-border research integrity.

## 2. Common Rule (45 CFR 46) Applicability Analysis

### 2.1. Jurisdictional Triggers (45 CFR § 46.101(a))
The Common Rule applies exclusively to:
- Research conducted, supported, or otherwise subject to regulation by any federal department or agency that has adopted the Common Rule (e.g., HHS, NSF, DoD, DoE).
- Institutions that have entered into a Federalwide Assurance (FWA) with the Office for Human Research Protections (OHRP) binding all institutionally conducted research to Common Rule oversight.

**Evaluation for HelpUS LLC:**
- HelpUS LLC is a privately held corporation registered in the United States.
- The research is 100% privately sponsored and self-funded.
- No federal grants, cooperative agreements, or federal contracts fund this project.
- HelpUS LLC has not voluntarily filed an unconditioned FWA mandating universal Common Rule applicability to non-federal commercial software benchmarking.
- **Conclusion**: US federal statutory IRB oversight is **not triggered**.

### 2.2. Exemption Classification under 45 CFR § 46.104(d)(2)
Even under hypothetical federal nexus, the study protocol satisfies the criteria for statutory exemption under 45 CFR § 46.104(d)(2) (Educational tests, survey procedures, interview procedures, or observation of public behavior):
- *Criterion A*: Human participants act as expert reviewers assessing technical artifacts (Common Vulnerability Scoring System vectors).
- *Criterion B*: Any disclosure of human subject responses outside the research would NOT reasonably place the subjects at risk of criminal or civil liability, nor be damaging to their financial standing, employability, educational advancement, or reputation.
- *Criterion C*: All expert evaluation data is pseudonymous and blinded; no confidential corporate or classified network configurations are evaluated.

## 3. FDA Regulations (21 CFR Parts 50 & 56) Applicability

FDA jurisdiction applies to clinical investigations of drugs, biologics, medical devices, and food additives under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.
- HelpUS CVSS AI is an automated vulnerability triage and scoring engine for software and computer networks.
- It is not a medical device, clinical diagnostic tool, or digital health application regulated under 21 CFR § 801/803/812.
- **Conclusion**: FDA IRB regulations are **entirely inapplicable**.

## 4. International Coordinating Ethics Review Model

To ensure uncompromising academic credibility and respect for international publishing standards (e.g., IEEE, ACM, Springer Nature, Elsevier):
1. **Coordinating Ethics Body**: The research is submitted to a registered Research Ethics Committee (CEP) within the Brazilian Platform Brazil (SINEP), operating under the oversight of CONEP (Comissão Nacional de Ética em Pesquisa).
2. **Zero Commercial Bias**: The Brazilian public CEP system evaluates protocols based purely on scientific merit, participant protection, and ethical risk, with zero commercial transactional relationship ($0.00 review fee). This completely eliminates the peer-review conflict-of-interest vulnerability often associated with fee-for-service commercial IRBs ($3,000–$5,000 fees).
3. **Dual Language Transparency**: All core governance dossiers, informed consent documents (e-ICF / TCLE), and data management plans are maintained with English as the canonical scientific language, accompanied by certified Brazilian Portuguese regulatory dossiers for SINEP compliance.

## 5. Triggers for Reassessing US IRB Review

HelpUS LLC will immediately initiate formal commercial US IRB review (e.g., via WCG IRB or Advarra) if and only if any of the following triggers occur:
1. Receipt of US Federal Research Grants (e.g., NSF, DARPA, NIH, DHS).
2. Explicit, non-waivable requirement imposed by an academic journal editor or conference chair after formal submission with CEP coordinating clearance.
3. Modification of research scope to include classified, human biometric, or physical infrastructure penetration testing involving human subjects without synthetic abstraction.
