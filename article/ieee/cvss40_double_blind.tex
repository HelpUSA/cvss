\begin{abstract}
CVSS v4.0 Environmental metrics adapt vulnerability assessment to organizational deployment context, but operationalization remains evidence-intensive and difficult to audit. We present a deterministic, machine-checkable workflow that normalizes environmental evidence, resolves candidate metrics under explicit policy, enforces human authorization checkpoints, and exports structured traces. The scoring core achieves 100\% conformance across 486,472 FIRST reference vectors with zero violations over 10,000 metamorphic executions. In differential validation against an independent secondary implementation (\texttt{cvss4py}) across 486,472 vectors, the pipeline demonstrated 454,047/461,444 exact numeric score concordance (98.3970\%) with zero validity disagreements, where all 7,397 observed discrepancies match official FIRST reference scores, alongside 25,028/25,028 (100.0\%) syntax rejection concordance on malformed inputs under verified deterministic parity. To evaluate contract robustness, closed-world Schema v2 validation and a downstream governance override contract were established without modifying nominal policy formulas. Replaying the historical mutation complement under closed-world Schema v2 achieved 120/120 rejections (100.0\%), including 16/16 subobject injections previously accepted under open-world Schema v1. In a pre-registered transfer evaluation on an external quarantined held-out corpus of $N=938$ real-world advisories, the workflow classified 339 cases as nominal-calculable (36.1407\%) and executed 599 fail-closed rejections (63.8593\%) due to missing mandatory operational evidence, with zero data invention, zero autonomous overrides, and 938/938 traces validated across 3,232 machine-checkable diagnostic checks under deterministic parity. The workflow provides machine-checkable provenance and governance constraints under controlled conditions; independent human expert adjudication has not been completed (\texttt{human\_adjudication\_completed = false}), and confirmatory datasets C1/C2 remain sealed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Vulnerability management teams routinely face more findings than they can
remediate immediately. A severity score is therefore an important input, but it
does not by itself encode every factor that affects an organization's
remediation decision. The same vulnerability may affect an isolated
development workstation, a public identity service, a safety-relevant
operational technology component, or a redundant internal service. These
deployments can require different treatment even when the vulnerability's
intrinsic technical characteristics are unchanged.

CVSS v4.0 explicitly separates Base, Threat, Environmental, and Supplemental
metric groups \cite{first_cvss40,first_spec}. Base metrics communicate
characteristics intrinsic to the vulnerability, whereas Environmental metrics
allow a consumer organization to represent local security requirements and
modified conditions \cite{first_user_guide,first_implementation}. This
separation creates an opportunity for context-aware assessment, but also an
operational burden: analysts must locate evidence, interpret incomplete
information, document assumptions, and preserve the reasoning behind each
metric choice.

This paper investigates whether an evidence normalization and governance workflow can assist that process
without modifying CVSS or displacing human judgment. We use ``watcher'' only to denote the deterministic evidence-ingestion and normalization component of the architecture; it is not an autonomous scoring authority. ``Workflow'' denotes the complete end-to-end process, including evidence processing, policy resolution, trace generation and verification, and the mandatory authorization checkpoint.
The pipeline ingests candidate evidence, normalizes it into a structured scenario record, resolves
reviewable candidate metric values under deterministic policy rules, identifies uncertainty, and exports trace artifacts.
A human analyst remains responsible for accepting, changing, or rejecting each
recommendation.

The work makes eight contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item a strict scientific separation between specification-level arithmetic
conformance (486,472 FIRST reference cases; 10,000 metamorphic executions with
zero violations) and controlled workflow evaluation across curated scenarios;
\item an evidence normalization and human-in-the-loop governance workflow for operationalizing CVSS v4.0
Environmental assessment;
\item a trace model linking evidence, candidate metrics, uncertainty, and
review state;
\item a controlled evaluation dataset of curated scenarios exercising schema coverage (12 real NVD records, 18 synthetic cases, 22 priority shifts);
\item a closed-world Schema v2 contract that replaces open-world permissiveness, eliminating subobject injection gaps (16/16 rejections in replay) and establishing strict field and enum boundaries;
\item an auditable downstream governance override contract enforcing eight strict operational invariants, preserving nominal Equation~8 formula invariance and guaranteeing zero autonomous overrides;
\item an independent differential validation protocol evaluating 486,472 vectors against secondary comparator \texttt{cvss4py}, establishing 454,047/461,444 exact numeric concordance (98.3970\%) on calculable vectors and 25,028/25,028 syntax rejection concordance (100.0\%); and
\item an external held-out transfer campaign across $N=938$ real-world advisories quarantined prior to execution, demonstrating 339 nominal-calculable assessments, 599 fail-closed abstentions under missing evidence, zero data invention, and 938/938 verified traces across 3,232 diagnostic checks.
\end{enumerate}

The evaluation is deliberately descriptive. It measures artifact completeness,
traceability, and context-sensitive operational transitions. It does not claim
that the watcher predicts exploitation, produces an authoritative risk score,
or improves remediation outcomes in production.

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Severity and Environmental Context}

CVSS is an open framework for communicating vulnerability characteristics and
severity. CVSS v4.0 retains a Base metric group while distinguishing Threat,
Environmental, and Supplemental information
\cite{first_spec,first_user_guide}. Environmental assessment includes Security
Requirements and Modified Base metrics, allowing the consumer to represent
conditions that differ from the vulnerable system's default assumptions.

The need for contextualization predates CVSS v4.0. Fruhwirth and Mannisto
examined the use of context information to improve CVSS-based prioritization
and response \cite{fruhwirth2009}. Later context-aware approaches, including
CAVP, combined vulnerability attributes with additional operational signals to
support prioritization \cite{jung2022cavp}. These approaches motivate
automation, but they also show that context must be represented explicitly
rather than inferred solely from a Base score.

Howland argues that CVSS has often been used outside its intended role,
particularly when technical severity is treated as a complete vulnerability
management decision \cite{howland2023}. The present work addresses that concern
by retaining Base severity as a separate field and labeling the prototype's
priority output as an operational category rather than an official CVSS score.

\subsection{CVSS v4.0 Nomenclature and Scoring Mechanics}

The official FIRST specification establishes four formal nomenclature tiers depending
on the metric groups populated in a score vector:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CVSS-B:} Base metrics only, quantifying intrinsic vulnerability characteristics across Exploitability (AV, AC, AT, PR, UI), Vulnerable System Impact (VC, VI, VA), and Subsequent System Impact (SC, SI, SA).
\item \textbf{CVSS-BT:} Base and Threat metrics, incorporating Exploit Maturity ($E \in \{\text{Attacked}, \text{Proof-of-Concept}, \text{Unreported}, \text{Not Defined}\}$).
\item \textbf{CVSS-BE:} Base and Environmental metrics, incorporating consumer-specific context without threat intelligence.
\item \textbf{CVSS-BTE:} Base, Threat, and Environmental metrics combined, reflecting contemporary exploit activity alongside consumer deployment context.
\end{itemize}

Environmental assessment in CVSS v4.0 operates across two distinct mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Modified Base Metrics:} Allow the consumer organization to override intrinsic Base values when local defenses, privilege architectures, or compensating controls alter attack difficulty or impact. These comprise Modified Attack Vector (MAV), Modified Attack Complexity (MAC), Modified Attack Requirements (MAT), Modified Privileges Required (MPR), Modified User Interaction (MUI), Modified Vulnerable System Impacts (MVC, MVI, MVA), and Modified Subsequent System Impacts (MSC, MSI, MSA). When unmodified, each metric defaults to its underlying Base value ($MX = X$).
\item \textbf{Security Requirements:} Express consumer-side mission criticality for Confidentiality Requirement (CR), Integrity Requirement (IR), and Availability Requirement (AR), taking values in $\{\text{High}, \text{Medium}, \text{Low}, \text{Not Defined}\}$. CR, IR, and AR rescale effective impact weights during MacroVector evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

Under the FIRST CVSS v4.0 standard, scoring does not follow an informal arithmetic formula. Vectors are mapped into a 6-dimensional discrete MacroVector:
\begin{equation}
\vec{M} = (EQ_1, EQ_2, EQ_3, EQ_4, EQ_5, EQ_6)
\end{equation}
where $EQ_1$ captures Exploitability (AV/PR/UI), $EQ_2$ captures Complexity (AC/AT), $EQ_3$ captures Vulnerable System Impact scaled by CR/IR/AR, $EQ_4$ captures Subsequent System Impact, $EQ_5$ captures Threat ($E$), and $EQ_6$ captures Modified Subsequent System Impact. Each MacroVector corresponds to a precomputed Equivalence Set with assigned boundary scores. The final score is resolved through deterministic interpolation between adjacent equivalence boundaries.

Crucially, the workflow presented in this paper does not reimplement or approximate this algorithm. Instead, the workflow ingests local evidence, resolves applicable policy rules into valid Modified metrics and Security Requirements, and delegates calculation to the canonical FIRST-compatible scoring core (independently validated against 486,472 official reference vectors).

\subsection{Stakeholder-Specific Prioritization}

The Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization framework models
prioritization as a decision process that depends on the stakeholder and its
objectives \cite{spring2021ssvc}. SSVC uses decision trees and explicitly
rejects a single universal ordering for every vulnerability management
community. This perspective is compatible with CVSS Environmental assessment:
both require consumer-specific information that cannot be obtained from the
vulnerability record alone.

The workflow proposed here does not implement an SSVC decision tree. Instead,
it adopts two related principles. First, the evidence record must identify the
consumer context. Second, the decision artifact must remain inspectable so that
an analyst can understand which local facts changed the recommendation.

\subsection{Threat and Exploit Prediction}

EPSS addresses a different question from CVSS severity. It estimates the
probability that exploitation activity will be observed for a vulnerability
within a future time window. Research underlying EPSS demonstrates the value
of data-driven exploit prediction for allocating remediation effort
\cite{jacobs2020remediation,jacobs2021epss,jacobs2023epss}.

Threat probability and Environmental impact are complementary rather than
interchangeable. An exploit prediction model can inform the urgency associated
with attacker activity, while Environmental metrics express consumer-side
requirements and modified deployment conditions. The proposed watcher keeps
those concepts separate and treats time-sensitive threat evidence as an input
requiring freshness checks.

\subsection{Enterprise Patch and Risk Decisions}

NIST SP 800-40 Rev. 4 frames enterprise patch management as a lifecycle that
includes identification, prioritization, acquisition, installation, and
verification \cite{nist80040r4}. The guidance also emphasizes coordination
between mission owners and technology management. This reinforces the need for
artifacts that communicate why a vulnerability was prioritized, not merely the
numeric value attached to it.

Recent empirical comparison of CVSS, SSVC, EPSS, and other scoring systems
shows that different systems can rank the same vulnerabilities differently
because they have different purposes and underlying signals
\cite{koscinski2025}. The implication for this work is not that one system must
replace the others. Instead, a defensible workflow should expose the signal
being used, the decision boundary, and the evidence supporting the result.

\subsection{Research Gap}

Prior work establishes the value of contextual information, stakeholder
decisions, exploit prediction, and enterprise prioritization. A remaining
operational gap is the reproducible transformation of distributed local
evidence into candidate CVSS v4.0 Environmental values. The contribution of
this work is therefore the traceable workflow around Environmental assessment:
evidence acquisition, normalization, recommendation, uncertainty signaling,
human review, and reproducible export.

\section{Research Questions and Design Goals}

The evaluation addresses seven research questions across two experimental phases: the baseline phase (evaluated under baseline Schema~v1, designated internally as historical campaign R5) and the contract-hardening and external-transfer phase (evaluated under Schema~v2 and the Governance Override Contract, designated internally as campaign R6). For reproducibility, frozen repository artifacts retain their original internal R6 research-question identifiers; the manuscript uses the continuous public-facing labels RQ4--RQ7.

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{RQ1:}] Can the workflow produce complete evidence and trace artifacts for each evaluated scenario?
\item[\textbf{RQ2:}] Does consumer-side context produce observable changes in an operational priority view relative to a Base-only view?
\item[\textbf{RQ3:}] To what extent does the trace schema deterministically encode evidence provenance, explicit uncertainty, policy transitions, and mandatory authorization states in a machine-checkable format?
\item[\textbf{RQ4:}] Does the machine-checkable verification contract maintain integrity and coverage when transferred to an external, quarantined held-out corpus of real-world advisories?

\item[\textbf{RQ6:}] Can a structured downstream governance override layer make operational deviations fully auditable while preserving the mathematical invariance of the nominal policy formula?

\subsection{Source Adapters}

Source adapters ingest vulnerability records and consumer-side evidence.
Vulnerability identifiers, Base vectors, Base scores, severity labels, source
URLs, and retrieval metadata remain distinct from local asset observations.
This separation prevents curated deployment context from being represented as
if it originated in NVD.

Consumer-side inputs may include asset role, exposure, privileges, security
controls, redundancy, confidentiality needs, integrity needs, availability
needs, and operational constraints. The prototype stores concise evidence
summaries rather than claiming direct integration with a production CMDB,
scanner, ticketing platform, or threat-intelligence system.

\subsection{Evidence Normalization}

The normalization layer converts heterogeneous observations into a stable
scenario schema. Each record contains identifiers, official Base information,
environmental context, candidate modified metrics, evidence summaries,
uncertainty flags, review state, and trace references. Required-field
validators detect omissions before article tables or reports are generated.

Normalization is important because an automated recommendation without a stable
evidence model is difficult to review or reproduce. The architecture must therefore
produce structured fields in addition to narrative text.

\subsection{Candidate Recommendation Layer}

The recommendation layer maps the normalized context to candidate
Environmental values. The candidate status is essential: the output is not
declared to be an authoritative score. The rationale field explains which
evidence supports each recommendation, while uncertainty fields identify
assumptions, stale observations, missing data, or conflicts.

The evaluated prototype focuses on deterministic evidence normalization, governance gates, and trace generation rather than the semantic accuracy of an unconstrained generative artificial-intelligence model. Any assistive language processing or watcher heuristics are strictly scoped to evidence ingestion support and remain outside the scope of semantic truth validation; candidate values are resolved through structured scenario logic, deterministic policy rules, and curated evidence schemas. No claim is made that an artificial intelligence model independently discovered or empirically validated ground-truth metric values.

\subsection{Closed-World Schema v2 Contract}
\label{sec:schema_v2_contract}
The original JSON schema formulation operated under open-world semantics, permitting undeclared attributes. As revealed by exploratory mutation testing, open-world tolerance allows arbitrary undeclared payload injections ($F3$). To enforce rigorous auditability, we formulated Schema~v2 as a closed-world contract. Under Schema~v2, all object schemas declare \texttt{"additionalProperties": false}, requiring all ingested properties to belong to the declared normative specification. Schema~v2 strictly enforces string enums (such as non-empty CVE identifiers matching \texttt{\detokenize{^CVE-\d{4}-\d{4,}$}}), structured object arrays for compensating controls, explicit nullability constraints, and reserved future-version field guards.

\subsection{Downstream Governance Override Architecture}
\label{sec:governance_override}
In operational environments, security analysts occasionally require policy-driven deviations from nominal algorithmic recommendations. To support these operational requirements without corrupting mathematical rigor, we introduced a downstream Governance Override layer. The override operates strictly post-scoring, leaving the nominal scoring function and prioritization formula (Equation~8) mathematically invariant.

The Governance Override layer enforces eight mandatory architectural invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{INV-G1 (Authorization Prerequisite):} Overrides require authenticated human identity, role authorization, and non-empty rationale.
\item \textbf{INV-G2 (Nominal Preservation):} Nominal score and calculated priority are immutably preserved in the audit trace alongside the override.
\item \textbf{INV-G3 (Mandatory Justification):} Every deviation must reference verifiable evidence identifiers.
\item \textbf{INV-G4 (Separation of Roles):} Automated normalization agents are strictly prohibited from approving overrides.
\item \textbf{INV-G5 (No Autonomous Override):} Automated components cannot initiate or self-approve operational adjustments; autonomous overrides are identically zero.
\item \textbf{INV-G6 (Formula Invariance):} Equation~8 parameters, weights, and scoring rules remain byte-level immutable.
\item \textbf{INV-G7 (Audit Reproducibility):} Given the same input trace and override payload, replay reproduces identical audit states.
\item \textbf{INV-G8 (Fail-Closed Default):} Any override lacking valid authorization or complete evidence fails closed to the nominal recommendation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Differential Testing and Held-Out Protocol}
\label{sec:diff_and_heldout_methods}
To verify the scoring core beyond self-consistency, we established an independent differential testing protocol comparing the primary engine with \texttt{cvss4py}~\cite{cvss4py} (pinned commit \texttt{9fe705c}) across the official FIRST corpus ($N=486,472$). To prevent data snooping, the protocol decouples numeric score concordance ($D_{\text{numeric}} = 461,444$) from negative syntax validation ($D_{\text{validation}} = 25,028$). Furthermore, to evaluate generalization, an external held-out corpus of $N=938$ real-world advisories was acquired from the GitHub Advisory Database~\cite{gh_advisory_database}, cryptographically hashed, and quarantined under commit-level locks prior to first evaluation.

\subsection{Human Authorization Checkpoint}

Every scenario requires review. The reviewer can accept, modify, reject, or
defer a recommendation. The workflow's governance model requires the final
record to retain the original candidate, the reviewer state, and the evidence
available at assessment time.

This checkpoint limits automation bias by making uncertainty and provenance visible.
It also supports later comparison between watcher recommendations and expert
assessments, which is planned as future work.

\subsection{Trace and Export Layer}

Each scenario produces a trace artifact containing source metadata, evidence,
candidate decisions, uncertainty, and review state. Dataset-level scripts
generate CSV, JSON, Markdown, LaTeX, validation metrics, and summary tables.
The exported artifacts provide a reproducible snapshot of the workflow state.

\section{Dataset and Experimental Method}

\subsection{Validation Structure and Separation}

The evaluation is structured into two strictly separated tiers:
specification-level conformance verification and controlled workflow evaluation.
Specification-level verification evaluates whether the scoring engine adheres to the
CVSS v4.0 mathematical standard and maintains formal monotonicity properties.
Controlled workflow evaluation assesses whether the evidence normalization pipeline successfully
structures evidence, flags uncertainty, enforces human authorization requirements as a governance constraint, and exports
reproducible artifacts across curated scenarios.

\subsection{Specification-Level Verification Design}

To establish an exhaustive reference-conformance-tested baseline prior to evaluating the operational
workflow, the deterministic scoring core was evaluated against the official FIRST reference
corpus Round~5, comprising 486,472 vectors evaluated against pinned FIRST commit
\texttt{d766d0c8ab4368b67bf27dd0e411eb481f05fdbb}. In addition, a metamorphic testing
campaign evaluated 10,000 executions across four metamorphic relations: MR-1 (monotonicity of
criticality), MR-2 (defense removal monotonicity), MR-3 (context permutation invariance),
and MR-4 (irrelevant control invariance). Specification-level verification establishes
arithmetic conformance to published standards; it does not evaluate contextual suitability or
operational effectiveness in production.

\subsection{Formal Specification of Metamorphic Relations}

Let $\mathcal{V}$ denote the space of valid CVSS v4.0 Base vectors, $\mathcal{E}$ the space of structured environmental evidence contexts, and $f: \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{R} \times \mathcal{M}$ the deterministic evaluation pipeline returning an Environmental score $S(v, e) \in [0.0, 10.0]$ and candidate modified metrics $M(v, e) \in \mathcal{M}$. The campaign (Campaign ID: \texttt{HELPUS-METAMORPHIC-10K-G9B}) evaluated 10,000 executions ($2,500$ per relation) across four formal relations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{MR-1 (Monotonicity of Criticality):} Let $e \in \mathcal{E}$ have security requirements $R \in \{\text{CR}, \text{IR}, \text{AR}\}$ ordered as $\text{L} < \text{M} < \text{H}$. Let $T_{\text{MR1}}(e)$ elevate one or more requirements to $R' > R$ ceteris paribus.
\begin{equation}
S(v, T_{\text{MR1}}(e)) \ge S(v, e)
\end{equation}
Violation criterion: $S(v, T_{\text{MR1}}(e)) < S(v, e)$.

\item \textbf{MR-2 (Defense Removal Monotonicity):} Let $e_{\text{def}} \in \mathcal{E}$ specify reachability under active defense or isolation ($\text{reachability} \in \{\text{ADJACENT\_ONLY}, \allowbreak \text{LOCAL\_ONLY}, \allowbreak \text{PHYSICAL\_ONLY}\}$). Let $T_{\text{MR2}}(e_{\text{def}}) = e_{\text{unprot}}$ remove network defenses ($\text{reachability} = \text{PUBLIC\_ROUTED}$, $\text{internet\_exposed} = \text{True}$).
\begin{equation}
S(v, T_{\text{MR2}}(e_{\text{def}})) \ge S(v, e_{\text{def}})
\end{equation}
Violation criterion: $S(v, T_{\text{MR2}}(e_{\text{def}})) < S(v, e_{\text{def}})$.

\item \textbf{MR-3 (Context Permutation Invariance):} Let $\sigma = \langle (k_1, v_1), \dots, (k_n, v_n) \rangle \in \text{Seq}(\mathcal{E})$ be an ordered serialization of evidence key-value pairs ingested by the workflow, and let $\pi \in S_n$ be an arbitrary permutation representing an alternate arrival or serialization order of the same logical evidence.
\begin{equation}
S(v, \pi(\sigma)) = S(v, \sigma) \quad \land \quad M(v, \pi(\sigma)) = M(v, \sigma)
\end{equation}
Violation criterion: $S(v, \pi(\sigma)) \ne S(v, \sigma) \lor M(v, \pi(\sigma)) \ne M(v, \sigma)$.

\item \textbf{MR-4 (Irrelevant Evidence Invariance):} Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the universe of evidence keys, $K_{\text{rel}} \subset \mathcal{K}$ the subset consumed by the environmental resolver, and $I$ an orthogonal evidence record whose keys satisfy $\text{keys}(I) \subseteq \mathcal{K} \setminus K_{\text{rel}}$ (e.g., unrelated operational logs, maintenance timestamps).
\begin{equation}
S(v, e \oplus I) = S(v, e) \quad \land \quad M(v, e \oplus I) = M(v, e)
\end{equation}
Violation criterion: $S(v, e \oplus I) \ne S(v, e) \lor M(v, e \oplus I) \ne M(v, e)$.
\end{enumerate}
These relations characterize verified invariants within the finite campaign ($2,500$ executions per relation, zero violations); they do not constitute an exhaustive universal mathematical proof across infinite input spaces.

\subsection{Unit of Analysis}

The unit of analysis is an environmental assessment scenario. A scenario
combines a vulnerability severity baseline with a consumer deployment context
and a candidate Environmental assessment. Scenarios are evaluated as workflow
records rather than independent observations of production risk.

\subsection{Hybrid Dataset}

The dataset contains 30 scenarios. 12 scenarios use
real NVD records carrying CVSS v4.0 Base information
\cite{nvd_cve_2026_4251,nvd_cve_2026_4252,nvd_cve_2026_4270,nvd_cve_2026_28490,nvd_cve_2026_28498,nvd_cve_2026_29510,nvd_cve_2026_29513,nvd_cve_2026_29520,nvd_cve_2026_29521,nvd_cve_2026_3644,nvd_cve_2026_4224,nvd_cve_2026_4253}. The remaining 18 scenarios are curated
synthetic cases designed to exercise contextual differences.

The hybrid design provides two kinds of control. Real NVD records preserve
authentic vulnerability identifiers, Base vectors, and source metadata.
Synthetic cases allow deliberate variation of context without implying that
the scenario corresponds to an observed production deployment.

The Environmental context associated with the NVD rows is curated locally.
NVD supplies vulnerability information, not the consumer organization's asset
criticality, compensating controls, or mission requirements. The manuscript
and dataset label this boundary explicitly.

\subsection{Scenario Construction}

Scenario construction follows four steps. First, the pipeline selects or
creates a Base vulnerability record. Second, it assigns a consumer context
template describing asset role, exposure, and security requirements. Third, it
generates candidate modified metrics and a rationale. Fourth, it exports an
uncertainty flag and review-required state.

The synthetic scenarios are not intended to simulate the statistical
distribution of enterprise assets. They are controlled test cases for the
workflow schema and validators. Similarly, the NVD sample is time-bounded and
is not claimed to represent the population of CVSS v4.0 vulnerabilities.
The benchmark is a verification corpus rather than a statistical sample. Its purpose is to exercise the trace schema, deterministic policy logic, verification mechanisms, and governance gates across heterogeneous asset profiles. It is not intended to support population-level inference, and no statistical generalization claim in this study depends on the corpus size.

\subsection{Measures}

Evidence coverage is the percentage of scenarios containing the required
evidence representation. Trace completeness is the percentage containing the
required per-scenario trace structure. Review coverage reports the presence of
a human-review state. Uncertainty coverage reports whether uncertainty is
represented explicitly.

Context sensitivity is measured by comparing the Base-only operational priority
$P_{\text{base}}$ with the Environmental-aware operational priority $P_{\text{env}}$.
Priorities belong to the ordered set:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P} = \langle \text{defer}, \text{low}, \text{medium}, \text{high}, \text{critical} \rangle
\end{equation}
with the bijective ordinal mapping $g: \mathcal{P} \to \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ defined as:
\begin{equation}
g(p) = \begin{cases}
0, & p = \text{defer} \\
1, & p = \text{low} \\
2, & p = \text{medium} \\
3, & p = \text{high} \\
4, & p = \text{critical}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The base priority $P_{\text{base}} \in \{\text{defer}, \text{low}, \text{medium}, \text{high}, \text{critical}\}$ is mapped from the official qualitative severity of the Base score $S_{\text{base}}$ via $P_{\text{base}} = \text{severity}(S_{\text{base}})$. Consumer-side operational prioritization derives the contextual priority $P_{\text{env}}$ via a deterministic policy function $h(P_{\text{base}}, e)$ defined over the deployment context $e$:
\begin{equation}
g(P_{\text{env}}) = \min(4, \max(0, g(P_{\text{base}}) + w_{\text{context}}(e) + w_{\text{control}}(e)))
\end{equation}
where $w_{\text{context}}(e) \in \{-2, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ is determined by the deployment environment profile ($+3$ for public API gateways, $+2$ for customer-facing web applications, identity providers, backend databases, and OT monitoring gateways, $+1$ for developer workstations, $0$ for internal administrative consoles, and $-2$ for isolated lab systems), and $w_{\text{control}}(e) \in \{-1, 0\}$ reflects active compensating controls ($-1$ when controls such as network isolation, WAF, or multi-factor authentication are present; $0$ when unconstrained).

These weights encode explicit policy design choices rather than parameters estimated from data. Section VI-I therefore evaluates deterministic local sensitivity within the declared discrete weight domain rather than statistical uncertainty or predictive robustness.

The Category Delta $\Delta_{\text{cat}}$ for a scenario is formally defined as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{cat}} = g(P_{\text{env}}) - g(P_{\text{base}})
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\text{cat}} > 0$ denotes priority escalation (up to $+2$),
$\Delta_{\text{cat}} < 0$ denotes priority de-escalation (down to $-3$), and
$\Delta_{\text{cat}} = 0$ denotes unchanged priority. Across the $N=30$ curated scenarios,
the sample mean category delta is:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\Delta}_{\text{cat}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \Delta_{\text{cat}}^{(i)} = \frac{6.0}{30} = 0.2
\end{equation}
confirming the exact empirical summary reported in the dataset metrics. The priority categories are prototype workflow labels, and their numeric difference is a custom category delta rather than an official CVSS specification metric. This formal separation explains why in Table~\ref{tab:nvd_cves} several CVEs exhibit a numeric score delta of $0.0$ while undergoing an operational priority shift ($\Delta_{\text{cat}} = \pm 1$): $S_{\text{env}}$ is governed strictly by the official FIRST MacroVector equivalence classes, whereas $P_{\text{env}}$ incorporates consumer-side deployment exposure and compensating control policy weights outside the CVSS scoring engine. Furthermore, the corrected domain $w_{\text{context}} \in \{-2, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ and $w_{\text{control}} \in \{-1, 0\}$ mathematically reproduces the full range of observed deltas $[-3, +2]$, including the de-escalations high$\to$defer ($\Delta_{\text{cat}} = -3$ in SCN-SYN-014) and medium$\to$defer ($\Delta_{\text{cat}} = -2$ in SCN-SYN-022) resulting from isolated non-production deployment ($w_{\text{context}} = -2, w_{\text{control}} = -1$).

The workflow deterministically encodes evidence provenance, uncertainty, and human authorization states in structured trace artifacts rather than exporting only an ungrounded score or category. Across all 30 canonical scenarios (12 curated NVD records and 18 synthetic test cases, with 12 out-of-scope legacy files segregated without benchmark contamination), the verifier demonstrated 30/30 canonical PASS (canonical failures = 0, overall status = PASS), validating 464 discrete checks across three categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Structural Integrity (240 checks, 0 failures):} Verified top-level schema conformance, required nested environmental evidence structures, mandatory boundary disclaimers, explicit uncertainty representation, and strict enforcement of the non-autonomous human review gatekeeper status (\texttt{human\_review\_status = "required"}).
    \item \textbf{Arithmetic and Policy Recomputation (128 checks, 0 failures):} Recomputed Base priority mapping $P_{\text{base}} = f(\text{Severity})$, Category Delta arithmetic $\Delta_{\text{cat}} = g(P_{\text{env}}) - g(P_{\text{base}})$, ordinal bounded range clamp $g(P_{\text{env}}) \in [0, 4]$, and nominal policy profile lookup decoupled via the canonical policy module (\texttt{core.cvss40\_environmental\_policy}).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Artifact Consistency (96 checks, 0 failures):} Enforced epistemological source separation (NVD Base scores recomputed via the canonical parser/scorer \texttt{core.cvss40}, whereas synthetic scenarios strictly validated CVSS v4.0 vector grammar and uncertainty disclaimers without treating assigned vectors as official reference specification) and cross-referenced all metadata against the canonical scenario dataset.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation recorded 34 structured schema limitations, isolated from failures: 30 instances reflect that the baseline Schema~v1 provides narrative evidence fields rather than atomic, machine-addressable linkage between each candidate modified metric and its corresponding evidence item; and four instances correspond to curated deployment adjustments in NVD scenarios (\texttt{SCN-NVD-001}, \texttt{SCN-NVD-006}, \texttt{SCN-NVD-007}, and \texttt{SCN-NVD-008}).

Specifically, the framework deterministically verifies Base-priority mapping, Category-Delta arithmetic, and nominal policy lookup. Operational priority matches the nominal policy profile in 26 of 30 scenarios. In four curated NVD scenarios (SCN-NVD-001, SCN-NVD-006, SCN-NVD-007, and SCN-NVD-008), deployment-specific constraints recorded in narrative evidence introduce a curated adjustment; the verifier records this as a schema limitation rather than treating the adjustment as silently recomputed from structured weights. In these four cases, the stored operational value is not deterministically recomputable from structured sub-weights because the local trade-off is recorded in narrative prose; the verification instead validates nominal policy derivation combined with declared curated deviation and consistency cross-validation.

These results provide empirical evidence for RQ3, demonstrating machine-checkable conformance with declared provenance, schema, policy-recomputation, uncertainty, and vector-validity constraints. Crucially, this conformance does not establish semantic correctness of the contextual choices, nor does it replace independent expert adjudication.

\subsection{Contextual Priority Sensitivity Stress Test}

To evaluate the operational stability of nominal prioritization decisions under local contextual weight uncertainty, we execute a deterministic sensitivity stress test across the 30 canonical benchmark scenarios (12 curated NVD records and 18 synthetic test cases). Because organizational environmental policy in our framework is purely deterministic and rule-based rather than estimated from training data, standard machine learning cross-validation (such as Leave-One-Environment-Out) is methodologically inapplicable; instead, local weight perturbation provides an appropriate, conservative assessment of operational stability.

The stress test perturbs policy weights strictly within the declared specification boundaries: $w_{\text{context}} \in \{-2, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ and $w_{\text{control}} \in \{-1, 0\}$. For each scenario, context weight is systematically varied across its immediate ordinal neighbors in the ordered domain (nearest lower, nominal, and nearest upper value, bounded by $[-2, 3]$), while compensating control weight is evaluated under both nominal and flipped binary states ($-1 \leftrightarrow 0$). The full evaluation grid spans 166 combinations across the 30 scenarios, comprising 30 nominal configurations and 136 strictly perturbed cases (56 on NVD, 80 on synthetic). All ordinal shifts ($\Delta_p = g(P_{\text{env}}^{\text{pert}}) - g(P_{\text{env}}^{\text{nominal}})$) are measured strictly against the unperturbed nominal policy baseline ($B4_{\text{nominal}}$); observed stored priorities ($B4_{\text{observed}}$) are preserved separately for descriptive traceability and are not used as an empirical reference baseline.

\subsection{Schema v2 Closed-World Compatibility and Replay (T2/T2R, T3)}
\label{sec:r6_t2_t3}
To address RQ5, we evaluated backward compatibility and mutation detection under the closed-world Schema~v2 contract. In the compatibility evaluation (Experiment T2/T2R), all 30 traces from the historical 30-scenario controlled benchmark were evaluated against Schema~v2. As formalized in protocol deviation R6-DEV-001, 30/30 historical traces were classified as \texttt{V1\_VALID\_V2\_INVALID} (0/30 valid under v2), demonstrating that Schema~v2 successfully rejects uncanonicalized legacy formats: 12 NVD records were rejected due to \texttt{INVALID\_TYPE} (narrative string in \texttt{compensating\_controls} rather than structured object arrays), and 18 synthetic cases were rejected due to \texttt{INVALID\_ENUM} (empty string \texttt{""} in \texttt{cve\_id} rather than null or valid CVE pattern). No undeclared fields were observed.

In the historical mutation replay campaign (Experiment T3), all 120 perturbations from the verifier-agnostic complement were resubmitted under Schema~v2. Replay achieved 120/120 rejections (100.0\%), compared with 83/120 under open-world Schema~v1. Crucially, in mutation family $F3$ (subobject injection), Schema~v2 achieved 16/16 rejections (100.0\%), in stark contrast to 0/16 under Schema~v1. This confirms the closed-world hypothesis: prohibiting undeclared properties systematically eliminates silent injection vulnerabilities without modifying scoring logic.

\subsection{Novel Mutation Campaign and Attribution Decoupling (T4, T4R, NF2-R)}
\label{sec:r6_t4}
To evaluate Schema~v2 boundaries against domain-specific faults, we designed six novel mutation families ($NF1$--$NF6$): extension unnamespaced keys ($NF1$), core-field shadowing in extensions ($NF2$), reserved keyword injection ($NF3$), nested array undeclared properties ($NF4$), explicit null on non-nullable scalars ($NF5$), and schema downgrade header injections ($NF6$).

In the initial execution (Experiment T4, $N=180$), all 180 mutants were rejected by Schema~v2. However, methodological auditing revealed that all 30 source parents were legacy traces already invalid under v2; thus, causal fault detection was confounded by pre-existing parent invalidity. In accordance with protocol deviation R6-DEV-002, we preregistered and executed versioned rerun R6.1-T4R on 15 deterministically canonicalized parents ($N=90$, 15 parents $\times$ 6 families).

In R6.1-T4R, the verifier achieved 75/90 attributable rejections (83.33\% overall): $NF1$, $NF3$, $NF4$, $NF5$, and $NF6$ each achieved 15/15 attributable rejections (100.0\%). In family $NF2$, 15/15 mutants were accepted by the primary schema because extensions allow arbitrary sub-objects under their isolated namespace. To test this specific boundary without confounding, we executed companion experiment R6.1-T4R-NF2R ($N=15$), verifying that when core fields are shadowed directly within the closed-world root, 15/15 are rejected under error category \texttt{UNKNOWN\_FIELD}. Methodologically, T4R ($75/90$) and NF2-R ($15/15$) represent decoupled test suites and are retained as separate denominators rather than summed into an invalid aggregate.

\subsection{Governance Override Invariants and Representability (T5, T5-R, T6)}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Completeness Versus Correctness}

The strongest result is artifact completeness. Every scenario passed evidence
and trace validation. This is necessary for reproducibility, but it is not
sufficient for correctness. A completely documented recommendation can still
contain an incorrect metric interpretation. Future expert evaluation must
therefore assess semantic agreement separately from schema completeness.

This distinction is particularly important for automated workflows.
Fluent narrative is not evidence of correctness. The architecture requires
structured sources, uncertainty fields, and human authorization checkpoints to reduce the risk
that generated explanations are mistaken for authoritative decisions.

Of the 34 schema limitations identified by the independent trace verifier, 30 arise because evidence is represented through narrative fields rather than atomic, machine-addressable records linked to individual modified metrics. The remaining four correspond to the curated baseline NVD scenarios (\texttt{SCN-NVD-001}, \texttt{SCN-NVD-006}, \texttt{SCN-NVD-007}, and \texttt{SCN-NVD-008}) in which deployment-specific narrative adjustments cannot be deterministically reconstructed from the structured policy weights alone (distinct from the representability evaluation in T6, which focused on structural transformation versus missing operational evidence across cases 001, 003, 007, and 011). This is an architectural limitation rather than an arithmetic verification failure. A future schema revision should require each candidate modified metric to reference a structured evidence record through a stable evidence identifier, extending machine-checkability to the evidence-to-metric boundary and reducing reliance on narrative-only adjustments.

The empirical results of the randomized mutation complement directly motivated our architectural formulation of Schema~v2 as a closed-world contract. In the R6 campaign, closed-world Schema~v2 was evaluated not as a retroactive bugfix of R5, but as a distinct governance hypothesis: prohibiting undeclared properties systematically eliminates subobject injections (16/16 rejections in replay) and enforces rigorous field typing. Similarly, the downstream governance override contract establishes an auditable bridge between operational requirements and formal scoring, ensuring that deviations require explicit human authorization while Equation~8 nominal weights remain mathematically invariant.

\subsection{Relationship to Other Prioritization Signals}

The workflow complements rather than replaces exploit prediction and
stakeholder decision models. EPSS can contribute time-sensitive threat
probability. SSVC can contribute stakeholder-specific decision logic. Asset
management can contribute exposure and mission context. CVSS Environmental
metrics provide a standardized location for selected consumer-side conditions.

A practical implementation could ingest those signals while preserving their
distinct meanings. Combining them into an unlabeled composite number would
reduce interpretability. The proposed artifact instead records the evidence
source and the role it played in the recommendation.

\subsection{Operational Use}

An organization could deploy the workflow between vulnerability ingestion and
ticket creation. New CVE data would initialize the Base record. Asset and
control evidence would populate the context model. The watcher would prepare a
candidate assessment and route it to an analyst. After review, the approved
record could inform remediation queues and service-level decisions.

The current prototype does not implement this production integration. Its
value is the validated data contract and trace package needed before such an
integration can be evaluated safely.

\subsection{Governance and Automation Boundaries}

The watcher must not silently overwrite official Base information, convert
missing data into confident values, or treat a recommendation as reviewed.
Sources should carry retrieval timestamps and provenance. Time-sensitive
evidence should expire or be flagged for refresh. Conflicting observations
should increase uncertainty rather than be resolved invisibly.

Human authority should also be represented as data. A production version
would record reviewer identity or role, timestamp, decision, changes, and
rationale. Double-blind research artifacts omit personal identifiers, but the
workflow design retains the concept of accountable review.

The governance model strictly isolates active operational assets from unexecuted
confirmatory protocols. The confirmatory protocol G10B remains explicitly in a
defined but unexecuted state. Correspondingly, held-out confirmatory benchmark
datasets C1 (real-world cases) and C2 (adversarial/metamorphic cases) remain
sealed and unexecuted under a pre-registered fail-closed gatekeeper
(\texttt{scripts/g10b\_confirmatory\_gate.py}) to enforce a strict no-retuning
constraint. They will not be inspected or executed without formal gatekeeper
release and explicit operational authorization.

Furthermore, independent human expert adjudication has not yet been concluded. The research
package includes a pre-registered blinded evaluation protocol (\texttt{article/expert\_review/}),
but claims no completed expert agreement or inter-rater reliability. The empirical results
presented in this paper reflect solely the descriptive evaluation of the 30 curated scenarios.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\subsection{Construct Validity}

Evidence coverage and trace completeness measure the presence of artifacts,
not their truth. Operational priority is a prototype category and is not an
official CVSS score. The evaluation therefore avoids interpreting the category
delta as a measurement of risk reduction.

\subsection{Internal Validity}

Scenario logic and context assignment were curated by the project. This introduces
construction bias (endogenous validation): because the 30 scenarios (12 real NVD records
with CVSS v4.0 data and 18 curated synthetic cases) were designed to exercise schema coverage,
field boundaries, and priority transitions, the 100.00\% evidence coverage, 100.00\% trace
completeness, and observed priority transitions reflect designed capability rather than an
independent natural distribution. Similarly, the ablation results evaluate internal benchmark
consistency under a shared governance framework; the agreement between nominal policy and stored
operational priority represents component consistency rather than independent external validation.
Furthermore, the mutation testing operators represent intentionally selected perturbations against
declared schema invariants; high detection demonstrates verifier sensitivity to those injected fault
classes only, and does not establish completeness against unknown fault classes or semantic correctness
of the evidence. Similarly, the contextual priority sensitivity stress test evaluates deterministic local stability under valid parameter perturbations bounded by the declared specification domain; profile weights represent deterministic design choices rather than estimated random variables, and the results do not estimate population robustness, calibrated uncertainty, or statistical generalization. They demonstrate that the workflow schema, ingestion pipeline,
and governance gates function as designed across diverse asset profiles, but do not establish
independent empirical proof of predictive superiority, operational accuracy, or real-world
risk reduction. Independent expert adjudication is required to test whether analysts agree
with candidate metrics.

The verifier-agnostic randomized mutation complement is subject to specific methodological threats. Although fully reproducible under \texttt{seed = 20261021}, evaluating a single seed does not characterize the full space of possible random perturbations. The six representative scenarios constitute a controlled verification subset rather than a statistical sample of enterprise vulnerabilities. Furthermore, while the generator operates independently without verifier assertion maps, the mutation family taxonomies were defined by the authors; generation is verifier-agnostic, but this does not render the evaluation unbiased. In addition, the 37 accepted perturbations lack an independent semantic oracle, precluding definitive determination of whether accepted cases represent false negatives. Rejection rates measure verifier sensitivity within the evaluated randomized mutation set; they do not measure semantic correctness, nor do they establish completeness, exhaustive fault coverage, or population-level generalization.

\subsection{External Validity}

Although the benchmark evaluation was expanded in R6 to include an external held-out corpus of $N=938$ real-world advisories from the GitHub Advisory Database, external validity remains bounded. The 938 advisories represent open-source software vulnerabilities and do not capture proprietary operational technology (OT), industrial control systems (ICS), or complex enterprise cloud service meshes. In addition, 599 advisories were rejected fail-closed due to absent environmental evidence, demonstrating that without active telemetry integration, operational applicability is constrained. Consequently, our findings demonstrate architectural feasibility, deterministic verification, and fail-closed safety rather than universal operational effectiveness across arbitrary enterprise infrastructures.

\subsection{Conclusion Validity}

The evaluation is descriptive and does not perform hypothesis testing.
It does not compare remediation time, exploit outcomes, analyst workload, or
prediction accuracy against a control group. The reported percentages describe
the current artifact package only.

\section{Operational Adoption Path}

A staged adoption process can reduce risk. In the first stage, the watcher runs
in observation mode and produces recommendations that do not affect
remediation queues. Analysts compare the evidence package with their normal
assessment. In the second stage, accepted recommendations may pre-populate
tickets while analysts remain responsible for approval. In the third stage,
organizations can measure review time, disagreement rates, stale-evidence
frequency, and decision consistency.

Expert evaluation should use multiple reviewers and a blinded scenario set.
Agreement can be measured per Environmental metric and per final operational
category. Review effort should be recorded independently of agreement so that
automation does not appear beneficial merely because it encourages rapid
acceptance.

A production study should also monitor reversals. When new threat or asset
evidence arrives, the workflow should generate a new trace version rather than
overwrite the previous assessment. This would allow researchers to examine
whether recommendations change for defensible reasons and whether uncertainty
flags predict later revisions.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper has presented a deterministic, machine-checkable evidence normalization, governance, and traceability workflow for operationalizing CVSS v4.0 Environmental metrics. The architecture strictly isolates official Base information from consumer deployment context, evaluates candidate metrics under explicit governance policies, enforces human authorization checkpoints, and exports structured, machine-checkable trace artifacts.

The investigation demonstrated 100\% arithmetic conformance across 486,472 FIRST reference vectors and zero violations over 10,000 metamorphic executions. In differential validation against secondary implementation \texttt{cvss4py}, the workflow achieved 454,047/461,444 exact numeric concordance (98.3970\%) on calculable vectors and 25,028/25,028 syntax rejection concordance (100.0\%) on malformed vectors. Formulating closed-world Schema~v2 eliminated subobject injection vulnerabilities (16/16 rejections in replay), while a downstream governance override contract achieved 8/8 invariant passes without modifying Equation~8 nominal formulas. In external held-out transfer across $N=938$ real-world advisories, the workflow classified 339 cases as nominal-calculable and executed 599 fail-closed rejections under missing evidence, with zero data invention, zero autonomous overrides, and 938/938 traces validated across 3,232 diagnostic checks.

Strict claim boundaries remain: independent expert adjudication has not been completed (\texttt{human\_adjudication\_completed = false}), and confirmatory benchmark sets C1 and C2 remain sealed under gatekeeper protocol G10B. Future work will execute blinded expert adjudication, measure inter-rater reliability, and evaluate live telemetry integrations in production environments.

\subsection{Blinded Review Design}

Each reviewer receives a counterbalanced packet containing the Base vector,
Base score, Base severity, consumer deployment context, security requirements,
controls, and evidence. CVE identifiers, source URLs, watcher recommendations,
priority deltas, and rationales are withheld during the independent review.

Two deterministic packet orders reduce the likelihood that fatigue or learning
effects are confounded with a single presentation sequence. Reviewers record
Security Requirements, Modified Base metrics, an Environmental vector,
operational priority, confidence, evidence sufficiency, missing evidence,
ambiguity, and review duration.

\subsection{Agreement Measures}

The analysis plan reports raw metric-level agreement in addition to
chance-corrected measures. Fleiss' kappa or Krippendorff's alpha can be used for
multi-reviewer categorical agreement, depending on missing responses. Ordered
priority categories can be analyzed using weighted agreement. Review time,
confidence, evidence sufficiency, defer rates, and watcher override rates are
reported separately.

The operational priority remains a prototype category and is not an official
CVSS score. Agreement with the watcher is not treated as proof that either the
watcher or a reviewer is correct.

\subsection{Adjudication}

After independent responses are locked, disagreements are reviewed in a
structured adjudication stage. The adjudication record distinguishes
insufficient evidence, ambiguity, reviewer interpretation differences,
data-entry errors, watcher errors, specification interpretation issues, and
operational-priority framework issues.

This standardized protocol specification strengthens the human-in-the-loop design by defining
formal procedures to measure where automation assists analysts and where it requires correction. No expert
agreement results or human adjudication data are claimed in the present study.
